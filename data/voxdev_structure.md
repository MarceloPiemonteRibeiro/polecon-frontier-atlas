# VoxDevLit — Political Economy of Public Service Delivery (Ferraz et al., Sept 2026)

Senior editor: Claudio Ferraz. Co-editors: Anderson Frey, Thomas Fujiwara, Meera Mahadevan, Diana Moreira, Wayne Aaron Sandholtz.
Source: input/Bread IGC PolEcon/VoxDev_PoliticalEconomy_slides_with_transcript.pdf (70 slides; citations read from slide images).

## Framing
- Third wave of democratization: access expanded, quality did not (education, health, infrastructure).
- Government effectiveness rises with income even net of spending → "the failure is political".
- Two-way arrow: political institutions & incentives -> shape provision; delivery outcomes -> feed back into electoral politics.
- Why distorted policies persist: staying in power, self-enrichment, elite opposition (Acemoglu & Robinson 2006; Bardhan 2016; Pande 2020).

## Six findings
1. Visible, attributable benefits crowd out quality (cash transfers, roads, electrification over-invested; learning, clinical quality, maintenance under-invested).
2. Voters punish malfeasance more reliably than they reward delivery.
3. Who governs matters as much as the rules (representation + political selection).
4. Reducing voters' material dependence weakens clientelism more reliably than information alone.
5. Organised frontline providers block reform more effectively than diffuse future beneficiaries defend it.
6. Bureaucratic quality mediates whether political will translates into delivery (political will and bureaucratic capacity are complements).
Lessons: access vs quality distinct; who governs; reduce dependence not just ignorance; reforms survive only if they alter the equilibrium; outsiders often start the shift.
