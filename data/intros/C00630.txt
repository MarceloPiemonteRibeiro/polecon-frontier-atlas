Abstract
Evidence-based policy implementation relies on effectively disseminating evidence which, in turn, depends on the attributes of the messenger. We conducted a large-scale field experiment to examine the role of ideological alignment between knowledge brokers and local policy makers on policy adoption in Spain. An ideologically nonsalient research institution and ideologically opposing think tanks and newspapers communicated identical policy information to local policy- makers. Policy adoption increased by 65% when the informing institutions aligned ideologically with policymakers, while opposite-ideology informing institutions had no effect. Ideologically aligned institutions foster policy adoption more than prestigious, ideologically nonsalient ones.
However, the latter difference (29%) is not statistically significant, likely due to limited statisti- cal power. Think tanks and newspapers were equally effective when ideologically aligned. These results provide crucial evidence on the critical role of knowledge brokers’ ideology in fostering evidence-based policymaking.
Keywords: evidence-based, policy adoption, ideological alignment, RCT, policy brief, media.

Understanding how to improve the dissemination of scientific knowledge to policymakers is crucial for economic and social progress. Despite worldwide efforts to disseminate research findings and promote evidence-based policymaking (OECD, 2020), a significant gap persists between available evidence and the policies ultimately implemented (European Commission, 2022). While some knowledge brokers, like think tanks, are affiliated with specific ideologies, others are not.1 This raises two piv- otal questions: How does the ideological alignment between the institutions disseminating research and policymakers affect the adoption of evidence-based policies? And, are prestigious institutions with nonsalient ideologies the most effective in promoting evidence-based policies? To answer these questions, we conducted a large-scale field experiment collaborating with prominent and authori- tative institutions with opposing ideologies who disseminated research findings to a large sample of local policymakers.
Our main experimental design keeps the information provided to policymakers constant and ma- nipulates two key variables — the institution disseminating research evidence and the format of information delivery — to investigate their impact on policy adoption across three potential factors.
